Overview

A tactical shooter built in the open

Southern Spear is a first-person tactical shooter in the tradition of classic team‑based military shooters: realistic pacing, small teams, clear roles, and objectives that only fall to coordinated play. It is set in a fictional world inspired by Australia.

You fight across outback stations, dry creek lines and red‑earth country as a soldier of the fictional 3rd Battalion, Australian Commonwealth Regiment (3 ACR). Matches are Objective Assault: two teams fight over a chain of objectives that fall in order, round after round.

There are no loot boxes, no pay‑to‑win and no hero classes. Every weapon and every character is mechanically identical on both sides — only how they look changes, and only to the viewer.

Systems

Four pillars of the design

These are the intended systems from the project's design documents. They are what the roadmap is building towards, not features that exist in a playable build today.

01

Train

Training is a core progression system, not a tutorial. It is replayable, stores your best results, and unlocks multiplayer roles.

  • Five graded modules, from Induction to Leadership
  • Scored weapons and support‑weapon qualifications
  • Field skills, including medic and identification training
  • Leadership strictly gated behind prerequisites

See the training framework in detail

02

Operate

Objectives are taken by holding the ring. Opposing soldiers inside contest it, so the fight comes to you.

  • Objective Assault — sequential objectives, round lifecycle
  • Secure and Hold — capture and hold, ticket scoring
  • Radio procedure and tactical communication as first-class systems
  • Role‑based sections with data‑driven limits

See the operations model in detail

03

Progress

Six separate concepts, kept apart on purpose. That separation is what stops progression collapsing into a kill counter.

  • Service record and a displayed service rank
  • Role and per‑weapon qualifications as binary unlocks
  • Named commendations for specific achievements
  • Rewards for objectives, teamwork, revives and conduct — not for kill ratio
04

Adapt

Every map ships as data, not code. A layer bundles mode, time of day, weather, objectives, deployment, roles, respawn model and ticket allocation.

  • Five original map concepts
  • Objective Assault, Secure and Hold, Day and low‑light layers per map
  • Configurable modes without recompiling
  • Accessibility and settings available throughout

See the map concepts in detail

Train

Training and qualification framework

Five modules, each granting specific roles. Leadership cannot be reached by skill alone in a firing range, and a qualification you have earned is never invalidated by a server being down.

  1. 01

    Induction

    Grants basic rifleman access

    Movement, interaction, HUD, stance, communication and rules of engagement.

  2. 02

    Weapons Qualification

    Grants Automatic Rifleman, Marksman

    Familiarisation, controlled pairs, supported firing, reloading and fire‑mode selection. Scored.

  3. 03

    Support Weapons Qualification

    Grants Section Support Gunner

    Bipod handling, deployment, suppression and ammunition management. Scored.

  4. 04

    Field Skills

    Grants Medic

    Navigation, observation, friendly and opposing identification, objective interaction and basic medical treatment.

  5. 05

    Leadership Qualification

    Grants Fireteam Leader, Section Commander

    Fireteam command, waypoint and order placement, radio procedure and objective planning.

Role prerequisites

Rifleman and Grenadier are open after Induction. Support Gunner, Marksman and Medic are limited to two per section. Fireteam Leaders and Section Commanders are limited to one per fireteam and section.

Read silhouettes, not colours

Identification training is explicit, because contextual appearance is only safe if players are trained to read silhouettes and insignia rather than colour.

Stated failure conditions

Failure conditions are stated clearly before an exercise starts, and accessibility options are available throughout.

Map concepts

Five original environments, each planned to ship with at least an Objective Assault layer, a Secure and Hold layer, a Day layer and a low‑light layer. Layouts are designed from the gameplay brief — sightlines, cover rhythm, rotation distances — not surveyed from any real location.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

What is Southern Spear?

An original, free, community‑developed Australian‑inspired tactical multiplayer first‑person shooter. It is built on Unreal Engine 5.8.3 using the Lyra Starter Game as its foundation, and is in pre‑alpha development.

Is the game playable?

No. There is no public build and no playable release. The editor target compiles cleanly and the project is developed in internal builds, but nothing has been released for others to play.

Is there a release date?

No release date has been announced. The project publishes its phases and session log instead, and has not committed to a schedule.

Is the project associated with the Australian Defence Force?

No. Southern Spear is a work of fiction. It is not endorsed, sponsored, developed or approved by the Australian Government, the Department of Defence, the Australian Defence Force or the Australian Army. All units, forces and weapons are fictional, and no map reproduces a real base, installation or operationally useful site.

Is it connected to America’s Army?

No. Southern Spear is not affiliated with America’s Army or any similar title. It is an original project with its own fictional setting, units and artwork.

Will the game be free?

The project is being developed as free to play, with no loot boxes and no pay‑to‑win mechanics. Donations are not currently being accepted.

Which engine does it use?

Unreal Engine 5.8.3, with the Lyra Starter Game 5.8 vendored in as the multiplayer foundation. Unreal Engine is a trademark of Epic Games, Inc.

How can people follow development?

This website publishes the roadmap and a session‑by‑session changelog generated from the project's own documents. The website and its published documentation live in a public GitHub repository, and there is a Discord server for discussion.

Can people contribute?

Not at present. The game repository is private, and the game itself is not open source. The website and the documentation published alongside it are in a public repository.

Will there be community testing?

Community testing has not yet been announced or scheduled. The roadmap includes a phase for online hardening and load testing, and the Discord is where testing calls would be posted first.
